08 · Solace · Recovery companion

The 30-day companion.

A daily walk back to yourself. One short page a day, at your own pace. Nothing is stored; your place is kept in the page address.

Day 1 · Week 1 — You Are Safe Now

You came here. That took courage.

Whatever brought you to this page, you took a step most people are too afraid to take. That matters. You don't have to understand everything today. You don't have to fix anything. You just have to be here, and you are.

Today: drink a glass of water, sit somewhere comfortable, and let yourself simply have arrived.

The first step is the hardest. You've already taken it.

Day 2 · Week 1 — You Are Safe Now

It was not your fault.

You may be replaying it, asking how you missed the signs. Stop, just for a moment. You were not careless. You were targeted by people who do this for a living, with scripts refined on thousands before you. Blaming yourself is the scam's final trick. Don't give it that too.

Today: when the thought "how could I be so stupid" comes, answer it once, out loud: "I was targeted. That is not the same as being foolish."

You did not fail. You were deceived by a professional.

Day 3 · Week 1 — You Are Safe Now

The feelings were real.

Here is something no one tells you: the love you felt was real. Your heart did exactly what a healthy heart does — it opened, it trusted, it hoped. The person on the other end was a lie. Your feelings were not. That is why this hurts like a real loss. Because to you, it was one. The truth changes what it was — it does not change what it felt like. And those feelings took time and process to grow, so be patient that they don't vanish the moment you learn the truth.

Today: let yourself feel it without judging the feeling. Grief means you were capable of love. That is not a weakness.

The feelings were real. The person creating them was not.

Day 4 · Week 1 — You Are Safe Now

Protect what's left.

Gently, today, we tend to the practical. Not in panic — in care. Change the passwords they might know. If you shared bank details, tell your bank. Keep screenshots somewhere safe, then you needn't look at them again. This isn't about damage already done. It's about closing the door so you can rest behind it. And if you find yourself going back — re-reading the messages, checking if they're still online — be gentle with yourself. That pull is the scam still living in your nervous system, not weakness. You don't have to block everything on day one. Reduce, slowly. What you don't want to keep feeling, you do not need to keep watching.

Today: do one small protective thing. Just one. Then stop.

Closing the door is not fear. It's you, taking your house back.

Day 5 · Week 1 — You Are Safe Now

You don't owe anyone the whole story yet.

People may want explanations — family, friends, maybe people who warned you. You are not required to narrate your pain on demand. "I'm dealing with something and I'll talk when I'm ready" is a complete sentence. Protect your energy. You need it for healing, not for defending yourself.

Today: give yourself permission to keep your story until you choose to share it.

Your privacy is yours. Reclaim it.

Day 6 · Week 1 — You Are Safe Now

One person.

Isolation was part of how this worked — you may have been slowly taught to pull away from the people who love you. This week, we begin reversing that, gently. Not the whole world. One person. You don't have to confess anything. A simple "thinking of you" reopens a door.

Today: reach out to one person you trust. A text is enough.

The wall they built around you was deliberate. One window is enough to start.

Day 7 · Week 1 — You Are Safe Now

Rest.

You made it through the hardest week. The shock, the first truth, the first small steps. That deserves rest, not more effort.

Today: do nothing productive. Rest, eat something kind, sleep if you can. You've earned a soft day.

You survived the week the truth arrived. Be gentle with yourself now.

Day 8 · Week 2 — The Grief

You are allowed to grieve.

People may say "but they weren't even real." It doesn't matter. You lost someone you loved, plus the future you imagined with them. That is a genuine bereavement, and you are allowed to mourn it fully. And if you lost no money at all — if the wound was only to your heart — it still counts, completely. Emotional injury is injury.

Today: name what you lost — not just money, but the companionship, the hope, the mornings you looked forward to. Naming it is the start of releasing it.

Grief is the price of having loved. You loved truly. So grieve truly.

Day 9 · Week 2 — The Grief

The anger is welcome.

If anger is rising — at them, at yourself, at the world — let it come. Anger is not the opposite of healing; it's part of it. It means a part of you knows you deserved better.

Today: let the anger have a safe place — write it, walk it out, say it aloud where no one can hear. Don't aim it inward.

Your anger is proof you knew your own worth all along.

Day 10 · Week 2 — The Grief

Two losses, not one.

You're grieving two things at once: the person who held you, and the person you were when you believed in them. That second loss is quieter, but it's real. Be patient with how heavy it feels.

Today: be kind to the version of you who hoped. They weren't foolish for hoping. They were human.

You lost a future you could see. Of course it aches.

Day 11 · Week 2 — The Grief

Shame is a liar.

Shame whispers that you should have known, that everyone can see your mistake, that you are less now. Every word of it is false. Shame keeps victims silent — and silence is exactly what the people who did this depend on.

Today: notice one shameful thought and label it for what it is: "That's shame talking. It's lying to keep me quiet."

Shame on the people who deceive. Compassion for the ones deceived. Always.

Day 12 · Week 2 — The Grief

Say it out loud.

There is a strange relief in saying the true thing once, plainly. "I was the victim of a romance scam." It loses some of its power over you when it stops being a secret you guard.

Today: say it once — to yourself in the mirror, to a journal, or to a trusted person if you feel ready. Just once.

A secret kept in shame grows. A truth spoken in safety shrinks.

Day 13 · Week 2 — The Grief

Tears are not weakness.

If the tears come this week, let them. Crying is the body releasing what the mind can't hold. It is not falling apart. It is the wound draining so it can close.

Today: if you need to cry, find a private, safe place and let yourself. Then wrap up warm afterward.

Tears are not the breaking. They are part of the mending.

Day 14 · Week 2 — The Grief

A softer week is coming.

Two weeks. The rawest part is behind you, even if it doesn't feel like it yet. Next we begin to understand what happened — and understanding will start to set you free.

Today: rest again. Notice one small thing that felt even slightly lighter than last week.

You're still standing. That is not nothing. That is everything.

Day 15 · Week 3 — Understanding

This was engineered.

What happened to you was not random and it was not personal failure. It followed a method — rushed intimacy, a too-good story, manufactured urgency. These are tools, used the same way on countless people. You met a machine wearing a face.

Today: read back over it not as "my mistake" but as "their method." See the pattern. The pattern isn't about you.

You weren't gullible. You were up against a profession.

Day 16 · Week 3 — Understanding

They studied you.

The reason it felt so real is that they made it feel real — mirroring your words, your faith, your loneliness, your hopes. That's not your naivety. That's their craft, aimed with care at the most human parts of you.

Today: when you think "I should have seen it," remember: it was built not to be seen.

It felt real because someone worked very hard to make it feel real.

Day 17 · Week 3 — Understanding

The ask was infrastructure, not love.

The gift card. The "emergency." The locked funds. None of it was about you, and none of it was spontaneous. Those requests are machinery — the same scripts, the same fake crises, run on a production line. You were not chosen for love. You were processed by a system.

Today: separate the affection you felt from the machinery you were caught in. They are not the same thing.

The request was not random. It was infrastructure.

Day 18 · Week 3 — Understanding

Isolation was the weapon.

"Don't tell anyone." "They won't understand us." "It's just you and me." That wasn't intimacy — it was a cage, built so no one could reach you with the truth. The distance you feel from loved ones now was designed.

Today: notice that the loneliness was manufactured. Then take one more small step back toward someone real.

They isolated you on purpose. Reconnecting is how you win.

Day 19 · Week 3 — Understanding

You were targeted because you can love.

Here is the truth that undoes the shame: they came for you because you have a heart that trusts, hopes and gives. Those are not flaws. They are the best of you. Don't let anyone — including you — turn your kindness into the reason you "deserved" this. And the emptiness you feel now is proof of how deeply you can connect. That capacity was always yours. It was never theirs to take.

Today: name one good thing about your heart that they exploited. Then decide to keep it.

They weaponised your goodness. The goodness is still yours to keep.

Day 20 · Week 3 — Understanding

Begin to forgive yourself.

Not all at once. Just begin. The person who trusted, who hoped, who loved — they didn't do anything wrong. They were lied to. You can lay the blame down now. It was never yours to carry.

Today: say it gently: "I forgive myself for being human." Say it as many times as it takes.

Forgiveness isn't a finish line. It's a direction. Just face that way.

Day 21 · Week 3 — Understanding

Knowledge is armour.

You see it now — the method, the machinery, the manufactured love. That sight is permanent. No one will ever run this on you the same way again, because you know the shape of it. Your pain has become protection.

Today: notice that you understand something now that most people never will. That understanding is power.

What wounded you has also armed you. That is yours now.

Day 22 · Week 4 — Reclaiming You

Reconnect.

The relationships strained during all this can mend. The people who love you mostly want you back, not an apology. You don't have to explain everything — showing up is the message.

Today: reach toward one relationship that the scam pushed away. A call, a visit, a small honest sentence.

The bridge isn't burned. It's just been quiet. Walk back across it.

Day 23 · Week 4 — Reclaiming You

Trust yourself again — slowly.

You may feel you can't trust your own judgement anymore. You can. It will come back the way strength returns after illness — gradually, with use. Start with tiny decisions and let the confidence rebuild.

Today: make one small choice just for you and honour it. A meal you want, a walk you choose.

Your judgement wasn't broken. It was deceived. It's still yours, and it's healing.

Day 24 · Week 4 — Reclaiming You

Who were you before?

Somewhere under these weeks is a person with interests, laughter, a life. They're still there. The scam took up enormous space — today we give a little of it back to you.

Today: do one thing you used to enjoy before all this. Even ten minutes of it.

You existed, fully, before them. That person is waiting for you.

Day 25 · Week 4 — Reclaiming You

Your instinct was right.

Somewhere along the way, a quiet voice said something's not right. You may have silenced it — they trained you to. But it was there, and it was correct. That instinct is a gift, and it still works.

Today: thank the part of you that knew. Promise to listen to it from now on.

You weren't imagining it. Some part of you saw clearly all along.

Day 26 · Week 4 — Reclaiming You

Help can be received.

You've carried much of this yourself. You don't have to. A support group of others who've been here, or a counsellor who understands fraud trauma, can lighten what's left. Receiving help is not failure — it's wisdom.

Today: if you feel ready, reach toward one source of real support — a support group, a counsellor, a helpline, or OSB itself. Ask for help from real, empathetic people.

Letting others in is not weakness. It's how the strong heal faster.

Day 27 · Week 4 — Reclaiming You

You are not your worst week.

This will not always be the first thing people know about you, or the first thing you think about yourself. It is one chapter — a hard one — in a long story that you are still writing.

Today: finish this sentence for yourself: "I am also someone who…" — and make it about who you are beyond this.

This happened to you. It is not who you are.

Day 28 · Week 4 — Reclaiming You

Look up.

Four weeks ago, looking forward may have felt impossible. Notice, today, that a future exists again — even a small, ordinary one. That's not nothing. That's the horizon coming back.

Today: picture one good, ordinary day in your future. Let yourself want it.

The future didn't end. It just went quiet for a while. It's speaking again.

Day 29 · Beyond the 30 Days

Your story can be a light — when *you* choose.

One day, when you're ready — not before — what happened to you could protect someone else. Your voice could be the warning that saves a stranger. There is no obligation and no timeline. But know that your pain can become someone else's armour.

Today: simply notice the strength it would take, and that the choice is entirely, always, yours.

Break the silence — on your terms, in your time. Never anyone else's.

Day 30 · Beyond the 30 Days

This is day one.

You reached the end of the thirty days. But this was never a finish line — it's a beginning. You may not feel fully healed, and that's honest; healing keeps going. Carry these truths with you: it was not your fault, the feelings were real, you were targeted because you can love, and you are not alone.

Today: look back at how far you've come in thirty days. Then take the next step — gently, as your own person, reclaiming your life.

You came in wounded. You leave armed, awake, and still capable of love. Reclaim your life.
