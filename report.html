New techniques documented globally first by OSB

Social engineering fraud is not slowing. The $442B–$1.03T annual range (GASA 2024–25) represents confirmed and reported cases across all typologies. The true figure — accounting for non-reporting, cross-scheme operators, and emerging markets — is significantly higher. OSB field intelligence confirms that the operational sophistication of social engineering networks is increasing year on year.

In 2026, OSB documented two new globally-significant S-Pillar techniques. The first (IFN-001) defeated the standard advice to "verify independently." The second (IFN-005) defeated "I saw the money with my own eyes." Together they represent a new class of financial credibility engineering that existing fraud prevention guidance does not address.

Section 2 · RSEI Scoring Data

What the scores tell us.

Of all cases scored through the RSEI framework in the period, the average Core Score was 14.2 — falling within the High Probability Social Engineering tier. The S pillar showed the highest average at 4.1/5, confirming that script sophistication remains the primary operational tool of social engineering networks.

The E Pillar Independent Trigger was activated in 68% of all scored cases — meaning emotional exploitation was operating at a clinically significant level regardless of whether any financial extraction had occurred. This confirms the fundamental OSB position: emotional harm is real harm.

"E ≥ 4 in 68% of cases with zero financial loss confirms that the conventional definition of romance fraud — 'when someone loses money' — is dangerously incomplete."

John Dee · OSB Annual Report 2026 · Section 3 · New Techniques Documented

What OSB found first — globally.

The Proxy Caller Exploit (IFN-001) — The scammer provides real stolen account credentials. The victim independently calls the real bank and receives confirmation of a large balance from the automated system. The victim's own verification step was the trap. This defeats the primary fraud prevention advice in circulation globally. First documented by OSB.

The Facade Bank Page (IFN-005) — A fake bank portal interface showing fabricated wealth, displayed to the victim before any financial request is made. Distinct from IFN-001 — this uses visual fabrication rather than automated phone confirmation. The victim sees the "money" before being asked for anything. First documented by OSB.

Section 4 · Continental Distribution

Where it happens.

Social engineering operations have been documented on every continent, across dating platforms, social networks, professional platforms, and messaging applications. The victim base is global and crosses all income levels, ages and education backgrounds. Africa — particularly West Africa — remains the primary operational hub. North America, Europe and Oceania are primary victim markets. Asia is both a victim market and an emerging operational hub.

Seniors — people over 60 — represent the most financially impacted demographic globally. This is not because seniors are less intelligent. It is because they carry greater accumulated wealth, are more likely to be living alone, and were formed in an era when trusting strangers was safer than it is now.

Section 5 · Platform Accountability

Where scams begin.

In OSB case records, first contact most often begins on dating apps and social networks. Operators then move the conversation to private messaging apps, where platform safety tools can no longer see it. That move is where platform protection ends.

The techniques documented in this report expose specific gaps: identity checks that confirm a face but not intent, abuse reports that go unanswered, and no warning to people already in contact with a banned account. The US Romance Scam Prevention Act, signed in October 2026, will require US dating platforms to close the last of these gaps from October 2027.
