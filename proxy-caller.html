Romance Scam: The Proxy Caller Exploit –
You are not the target, you are the mechanism.

John Dee · OSB · April 8, 2026 · OSB Intelligence Dispatch · Scam Watch

You called the bank yourself. Real number. Right questions.

They confirmed the balance. You hung up feeling certain. That certainty was the goal.

Matter of fact, He couldn't make that call. Not with his accent. Not without leaving a trace. So he spent time love bombing someone who would make it for him — willingly, carefully, proudly.

When you confirmed that balance, you didn't make a mistake. You completed a step in a criminal operation you were oblivious of being a part of.

He wanted more than your money. He wanted your legitimacy. Your accent. Your clean record on the other end of a recorded bank line. That's how stolen funds move without resistance.

The money is real. It belongs to someone else. And your voice — clean, credible and careful is the key to move it.

You are not naive. You were engineered. Doing everything right was the trap. This is the layer most people never see in romance fraud:

It's not always "send me money."
Sometimes it's "help me confirm something"
Sometimes it's "help me move something small."

Sometimes the ask is so small, so reasonable, wrapped so carefully in the language of affectation of love — that it doesn't feel like an ask at all.

If this scenario feels familiar, understand where you are right now :

You are being positioned inside a money laundering chain. This is not one person improvising.

This is structured fraud — roles, scripts, timing. And somewhere in that structure, your phone call has already been logged as a successful step.

For the record, you are being groomed as a financial conduit to move stolen funds through the system.

You didn't miss something obvious. You did your due diligence. That's exactly what made this work.

So, don't be too hard on yourself — but don't ignore it either.

This feels familiar? Now that you can see it — step out of it .
