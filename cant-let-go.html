Romance Scam: I Know He Is A Scammer.
I Still Can't Let Go.

John Dee · OSB · April 26, 2026 · OSB Intelligence Dispatch · Scam Watch

He didn't win your heart in a day. It took time. It took process.

So there is no reason your feelings should disappear the moment you find out the truth. The truth changes what it was. It doesn't change what it felt like. Pats on the back. Be easy on yourself.

Going back through the pictures? Re-reading the messages? Checking if he's still online and all that jazz? You already know none of that helps. You do it anyway — because the absence feels worse than the reminder.

That's the trap inside the trap. The scam continues in your nervous system long after the conversation ends.

Here is what moving forward actually looks like:

You don't block him on day one. You reduce. Gradually. Confronting him won't give you closure; it will give him another opening.

Erase the digital presence. Every photo. Every saved message. Close the doors. What you don't want, you do not watch.

Fill the space. That void is proof you are capable of deep connection. That capacity belongs to you. It was never his.

And if the weight becomes too heavy — ask for help. Real help. From real people, empathetic people.

Matter of fact: You do not have to have lost money for this to count as damage. Emotional injury is injury.
