A Note From OSB

A Note to Romance Scam Victims

Anyone can fall for a romance scam under the right circumstances. You are not alone.

It's okay to grieve the emotional and financial investment you made. Painful experiences—even those we never chose—can still shape us and teach us.

Don't let this experience define your self-worth. You are still intelligent, brave, and strong.

Falling victim to a romance scam is not a reflection of lack of wisdom or intelligence as judgmental people think of it. It is the result of a cyber predator who carefully studied how to exploit trust, hope, kindness, loneliness, and love.

Forgive yourself. Holding on to self-blame only drains the emotional strength you need to heal and move forward.

Suicide is never the answer. Your life matters — to the people who love you and to the future that still lies ahead. As the ancient proverb says, "A living dog is better than a dead lion."

To Those Who Have Never Experienced This

Never judge what you have not experienced.

Judgment and harsh criticism only deepen the pain and isolation that victims already carry.

Romance scams are crimes of psychological manipulation. Their success is driven by deception, grooming, and emotional exploitation — not by a simple measure of intelligence. Anyone can become vulnerable under the right circumstances.

P.S. If you are having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, please reach out to a trusted friend or family member, a mental health professional, or a crisis service in your country. You do not have to carry this burden alone. Help is available, and recovery is possible.

John Dee

Founder, OSB

You did not fall for a trick. You fell for a person — or what appeared to be one. Romance scammers exploit the best of you — your capacity to love, to trust, to hope, to give. They study you. They mirror you. They build something that feels real because they invested significant time and skill in making it feel that way.

The feelings you had for this person were real. The love was real. The hope was real. What was not real was the person on the other side of it. OSB Solace is here to walk you through what comes next — at your pace, with no judgment and no rush.

The Six Stages

Your recovery. Your pace.

01 · Confusion and Disbelief · You know something is wrong — but the feelings were real. Both can be true. The feelings were real. The person creating them was not.

02 · Shame and Self-Blame · The most damaging stage — and the one the scammer depends on. You were not careless. You were targeted by a professional operation built to defeat careful, intelligent people.

03 · Anger · Anger is healthy. It means you are beginning to see clearly. Let it move through you — just do not let it become the destination. · 04 · Grief · You are grieving a person who never existed. That grief is real and it deserves the respect you would give any genuine loss.

05 · Acceptance · Acceptance does not mean it was okay. It means you have stopped fighting reality and started working with it. · 06 · Reclamation · This is not going back to who you were before. This is becoming someone who carries what they survived as strength — not shame.

OSB Solace · Recovery Journey

The Road Back

Not a cure. Not a shortcut. A structured journey — at your pace. Four phases. Each with something to read, something to reflect on, and one practical step forward.

Phase One

Accept

The hardest part is not finding out. It is sitting with what you found out. The mind wants to argue its way back to before. It replays conversations looking for the moment it was still real. There was no such moment. What was real was your capacity to love. That part is still intact.

You are not stupid. You are not weak. You were targeted. There is a difference — and it matters.

REFLECTION

Write one sentence that begins: "What I know to be true now is..."

ACTION

Tell one person you trust what happened. Not for their reaction. For your own voice to hear itself say it out loud. Spoken truth is the beginning of release.

Phase Two

Separate

Every photo you go back to is a door you are walking through. Every saved message is a room you are choosing to sit in. The operator is not in those rooms. The feeling is — and the feeling is lying to you about what it is.

Erase the digital presence. Not because you are deleting what happened. Because you are deciding what you are willing to keep feeding.

REFLECTION

What are you holding onto that is keeping you in contact with the pain? Name it specifically.

ACTION

Choose one thing — one folder, one conversation thread, one saved photo — and delete it today. Not all of it. One thing. Forward motion does not require a sprint.

Phase Three

Reclaim

Here is what the operator took: time, money, trust, peace. Here is what they could not take: your capacity for connection. Your willingness to love. Your openness. Those things are not liabilities. They are the best of you.

The void you feel is not emptiness. It is proof of what you brought. That fullness belongs to you. It was never his.

REFLECTION

Finish this sentence honestly: "Before this, I believed I was someone who..."

ACTION

Do one thing this week that you used to do before this happened. Not because it will feel the same. Because it reminds you who you were before someone tried to rewrite you.

Phase Four

Forward

Healing is not a destination. There will be days when something triggers the memory and it feels like starting over. It is not. It is a hard day inside a longer journey that is already moving in the right direction.

You do not have to forgive the operator. You have to forgive yourself — for trusting, for hoping, for being human in a moment when someone chose to exploit that. That forgiveness is yours to give, on your timeline, nobody else's.

REFLECTION

What would you say to someone else going through exactly what you are going through right now?

ACTION

Write that down. Read it back to yourself. You already know what you need to hear.

If the weight becomes too heavy to carry alone — please reach out to a professional. Real help, from real people trained to sit in this with you.
