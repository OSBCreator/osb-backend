Romance Scam Alert:
The Facade Bank Page

John Dee · OSB · March 18, 2026 · OSB Intelligence Dispatch · Scam Watch

It did not start with asking. It started with showing. A large balance — right there on the screen.

He walked you through it calmly, the kind of calm that comes from someone who appears to have nothing to hide.

Working. Building something. A man with substance, with means, with a future that included you.

You weren't being asked to give anything.You were being asked to help someone you trusted move money that was already his–from his bank account.

It felt natural. It felt safe. Then something went wrong.

A security flag. An account restriction, the kind that supposedly happens to serious people with serious money in serious places.

Hence the monetary request….But the money existed. You had seen it.

So the financial request feels small compared to what was supposedly waiting on the other side.

But that feeling of safety was not an accident. It was the architecture of the moment.

Part of the design of the scam.

Because the bank page itself was never real.

It was a staged interface designed to convince the victim that the money already existed.

Romance scams rarely look like scams while they are happening.

They look like someone you trust asking for help.

Caught in the web of this narrative? know this: It's a scam.

Mind you, clarity is not defeat. It is the first step to freedom. And the truth shall make you free.

Accept the truth, and begin the journey toward closure one step at a time.
